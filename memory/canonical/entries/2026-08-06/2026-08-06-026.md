## F-000 `7d3033022fab8078`

Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2023) reports pass@1 on HumanEval of 91% vs 80% for the then-SOTA GPT-4 baseline, a +11pp gain from the trial-error-reflection loop (arXiv:2303.11366v4).

## F-001 `691b74f6eacc0b29`

On ALFWorld (134 text-world tasks), ReAct+Reflexion completed 130/134 tasks vs ReAct-only which stalled; the paper reports an absolute +22% improvement over strong baselines within 12 iterative trials (Reflexion §4.1).

## F-002 `5016a8902d5d98e8`

On HotpotQA, Reflexion improved over baselines by +20%; with ground-truth context (CoT-GT) it lifted accuracy by 14% without access to ground-truth answers (Reflexion §4.2).

## F-003 `ccbdd85316bb9ecd`

Reflexion's ablation on the 50 hardest HumanEval Rust problems (GPT-4 base): base 0.60; self-reflection without test execution 0.52; test execution without natural-language reflection 0.60; full Reflexion (tests+reflection) 0.68. The reflection pass added +8pp only when paired with an external evaluator; 'reflect but no tests' performed worse than the base (Reflexion §4.3, Table 3).

## F-004 `3e53d4e63a90eb15`

On HotpotQA, an episodic-memory-only ablation (recent trajectory, no reflection) gained less than the full reflection step, which added an 8% absolute boost — the verbal reflection step, not just feeding back the failed trace, carried the gain (Reflexion §4.2).

## F-005 `4ab190f11c08150f`

Reflexion hurt on MBPP (Python): 77.1 vs GPT-4 baseline's 80.1 pass@1, attributed to flaky self-generated unit tests — the internal test suite's false-positive rate was 16.3% on MBPP vs 1.4% on HumanEval, so the agent prematurely accepted wrong submissions (Reflexion §4.3).

## F-006 `83f1964c6217fb0d`

ReAct (Yao et al., 2023), interleaving sparse 'thought' traces with environment actions, outperformed imitation and RL baselines by +34% (ALFWorld) and +10% (WebShop) absolute success rate; on WebShop ReAct's 40.0% SR beat Act (30.1%), IL (29.1%), and IL+RL (28.7%). Grounding reasoning in a Wikipedia API cut hallucination: among failed HotpotQA trajectories, hallucination was the cause in 56% of CoT cases vs 0% for ReAct (arXiv:2210.03629).

## F-007 `e5e61f2516f62242`

ReAct's ablation against Inner-Monologue-style prompts (ReAct-IM, replaying dense external feedback with no internal goal reasoning) shows internal sparse reasoning beats pure external-feedback replay: 71% vs 53% overall ALFWorld success — direct evidence about which reflection content adds value (goal-level re-planning) vs not (regurgitating observations) (ReAct §4).

## F-008 `af2b1630bfe751ef`

CRITIC (Gou et al., 2023) has the model validate its own output against external tools (search engine, code interpreter) and revise; the authors report it 'consistently enhances the performance of LLMs' across free-form QA, mathematical program synthesis, and toxicity reduction, concluding external feedback is 'crucially important' for self-improvement. Caveat: only the abstract was verifiable; per-benchmark numbers were not verified (arXiv:2305.11738, ICLR 2024).

## F-009 `b66006c1f5cda644`

Tyen et al. (2024, ACL Findings): LLMs cannot reliably find their own reasoning errors (even in objective, unambiguous cases), but can correct them when given the error location; feeding ground-truth mistake-location boosts performance across 5 reasoning tasks. A small classifier trained on out-of-domain data beat prompting a large model at mistake-finding — the bottleneck is error detection, and external/structured detection signals make correction reliable (arXiv:2311.08516).

## F-010 `a375ddea979ace49`

Training reflection into weights (rather than prompting it) shows large gains: SPOC (2025, preprint) — single-pass interleaved solution+verification model via SFT + online RL improved Llama-3.1-8B/70B by +8.8/+11.6pp on MATH500, +10.0/+20.0pp on AMC23, +3.3/+6.7pp on AIME24. S2R (2025, preprint) — teaching self-verification + self-correction via SFT and outcome/process RL lifted Qwen2.5-Math-7B from 51.0% to 81.6% with only 3.1k seed samples (arXiv:2506.06923; arXiv:2502.12853; both arXiv preprints).

## F-011 `6729b6c2eba5c724`

Retroformer (Yao et al., 2024) argues Reflexion-style verbal feedback 'does not reason and plan in ways compatible with gradient-based learning'; its learned retrospective model tuned by policy gradient improves agents over time and outperforms baselines that do not use environment rewards — the optimization of the reflection itself is the value-adding successor step (arXiv:2308.02151).

## F-012 `ab49a330f50c49ef`

Huang et al. (2024, ICLR) tested intrinsic self-correction (no external feedback) on reasoning: models 'struggle to self-correct their responses without external feedback, and at times, their performance even degrades after self-correction' (arXiv:2310.01798).

## F-013 `f3583d648963c6ab`

Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023) reports ~20% absolute average gain across 7 tasks, but on GSM8K the self-feedback loop gained ≈0pp (GPT-3.5 64.1→64.1, ChatGPT 74.8→75.0, GPT-4 92.9→93.1); the paper's explanation is that the model cannot detect its own math errors — ChatGPT's feedback was 'everything looks good' in 94% of instances — while with oracle (external) error feedback the gain became 5%+ (arXiv:2303.17651v2).

## F-014 `3900b567dfe90746`

Self-Refine's feedback-quality ablations: actionable feedback >> generic >> none (Code Optimization 27.5 vs 26.0 vs 24.8; Sentiment Reversal 43.2 vs 31.2 vs 0); on a weak model (Vicuna-13B) the refinement step fails entirely — the model cannot even emit feedback in the required format (Self-Refine §4, Table 2).

## F-015 `1051969642545360`

Liang et al. (EMNLP 2024) document 'Degeneration-of-Thought': once an LLM has established confidence, iterative self-reflection 'is unable to generate novel thoughts,' so reflection alone stagnates; their multi-agent debate outperformed it on commonsense translation and counter-intuitive arithmetic, and they find an LLM is 'not a fair judge' across different models (arXiv:2305.19118).

## F-016 `0b60fb3ea9f2562a`

A 2026 arXiv preprint (not yet peer-reviewed) recasts iterative self-correction as feedback control with a stability threshold (correct when error-correction-rate/error-introduction-rate exceeds a bound); across 7 models × {GSM8K, MATH, StrategyQA}, only o3-mini (+3.4pp), Claude Opus 4.6 (+0.6pp), and o4-mini (≈0) were non-degrading, while GPT-5 and four others lost accuracy; a verify-first prompt intervention turned GPT-4o-mini's −6.2pp degradation into +0.2pp (arXiv:2604.22273).

## F-017 `399d42c5feedc012`

A 2026 preprint (arXiv:2605.27559) finds the conditional miscorrection rate dominant (53–94%) across model families and benchmarks in a 'detection-without-correction' decomposition; another 2026 preprint (arXiv:2606.02646) finds a noise placebo matches self-correction on free-form math at 4× scale — suggesting much of the gain attributed to debate/reflection may be plain re-evaluation — and that 30 dense debating agents produce no more answer diversity than one. Both are arXiv preprints.

## F-018 `0cae2c2bdf2ef1dc`

A 2026 arXiv preprint (arXiv:2606.05976) shows the format/role of reflection matters to the point that the 'models can't self-correct' result may be partly an artifact: keeping an erroneous claim byte-identical but relabeling it from the model's own <thought> to an external role (user message, tool response, <memory>) raised explicit-correction rates by 23–93 percentage points (significant in 10/12 settings).

## F-019 `888b4004f537880f`

Renze & Guven (FLLM 2024) tested 9 LLMs × 8 self-reflection agent designs on a 1,000-question multi-domain MCQA exam and found all reflection types significantly improved re-answer accuracy (p<0.001) across all models, with information-rich reflections (Instructions, Explanation, Solution) beating minimal ones (Retry, Keywords, Advice), and even the plain 'Retry' agent improving. Protocol caveats from the paper itself: reflections were generated with the correct answer given as an external feedback signal (answer labels redacted afterwards), the task is single-step re-answering rather than an agent loop, the LSAT-AR exam dominated the gains, and strong models near the 100% ceiling compress the effect (arXiv:2405.06682).

## F-020 `9240d1f949e33e15`

Established synthesis: reflection loops produce large, replicated gains only when a reliable external evaluator grounds the reflection (unit tests/environment/exact-match/tools) — Reflexion +11pp HumanEval, 130/134 ALFWorld, +20% HotpotQA; ReAct +34pp/+10pp over IL baselines; CRITIC consistent gains; the Reflexion ablations pin this down (+8pp with tests, −8pp without).

## F-021 `6b4813b0dee8f914`

Established synthesis: pure intrinsic self-correction (no external feedback) on reasoning adds ≈0 or negative value — Huang et al., Self-Refine's GSM8K ≈0pp, Reflexion's test-omission ablation (0.52 < 0.60 base), and the 2026 stability-threshold preprint (only 3 of 7 tested frontier models non-degrading).

## F-022 `8fe5600483b3642b`

Established synthesis: the failure point in self-correction is error detection, not correction — Tyen et al. (location signals restore correction), Self-Refine's '94% everything looks good,' and the detection/miscorrection-decomposition preprint converge here; designs that externalize the critic (verifier model, tools, classifiers, verify-first prompting, RL-trained self-verification) improve reliability, while designs asking the generator to judge its own ungrounded reasoning add little or hurt.

## F-023 `33f44ff98f2913eb`

Uncertain/contested: whether 'self-reflection' per se (vs plain retry/re-sampling) contributes beyond the verification gate — Renze & Guven found even 'Retry' helped, a 2026 preprint finds a noise placebo matches self-correction on math, and another finds the 'can't self-correct' result is partly a chat-template role-labeling artifact; these are not yet reconciled. Renze & Guven's positive MCQA result and Huang et al.'s negative reasoning result are not comparable protocols (correct-answer-conditioned reflection + re-answer vs intrinsic correction).
