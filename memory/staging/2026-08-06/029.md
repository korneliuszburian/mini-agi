## S-000 (general)

Trust in automation should be calibrated: a person's trust should match the automation's actual capabilities; both overtrust (uncertainty ignored) and distrust (valid outputs ignored) degrade joint performance, and the design goal is 'appropriate reliance.' (Lee & See 2004, Human Factors 46(1):50-80; verified via metadata, full text paywalled)

## S-001 (general)

In AI-assisted decision making, calibrating trust case-by-case is a key to success: knowing when to trust or distrust the model lets an expert apply their knowledge, improving outcomes in cases where the model is likely to perform poorly. (Zhang, Liao, Bellamy 2020, ACM FAT*)

## S-002 (general)

People avoid algorithms after seeing them err even when the algorithm outperforms them, while being more forgiving of equivalent human errors (algorithm aversion). (Dietvorst, Simmons, Massey 2015, JEPG; verified at title/metadata level, full text not fetched)

## S-003 (general)

Modern neural networks are miscalibrated (overconfident), and temperature scaling — a single-parameter post-hoc step — is 'surprisingly effective at calibrating predictions' on most datasets; the standard cheap, model-agnostic production fix. (Guo et al. 2017, ICML)

## S-004 (general)

Conformal prediction lets any pre-trained model produce prediction sets/intervals with a user-specified coverage guarantee (e.g. 90% coverage), valid distribution-free and non-asymptotically. (Angelopoulos & Bates, arXiv 2107.07511)

## S-005 (general)

Conformal prediction for generative outputs: a calibrated stopping+rejection rule over LM sampling returns a small set of candidate answers containing at least one acceptable answer with high probability, and can flag individual spans (phrases/sentences) as non-hallucinated with statistical guarantees. (Quach et al., Conformal Language Modeling, ICLR 2024)

## S-006 (general)

Verbalized confidence: a GPT-3 model trained to express '90% confidence'/'high confidence' in words produces well-calibrated probabilities without using model logits and stays 'moderately calibrated under distribution shift.' First-party OpenAI result; basis for LLM verbal-confidence features. (Lin, Hilton, Evans 2022, arXiv 2205.14334; verified abstract)

## S-007 (general)

P(True)/self-evaluation: larger LMs are well-calibrated on multiple-choice and true/false questions 'when provided in the right format'; asking the model to evaluate the probability its own answer is correct yields encouraging calibration, improving when the model first samples many of its own answers. (Kadavath et al., Anthropic, arXiv 2207.05221; verified abstract)

## S-008 (general)

Semantic entropy (sampling-based uncertainty at the meaning level) detects hallucinations/confabulations across datasets and tasks without a priori task knowledge and without task-specific data, and helps users understand when they must take extra care with LLMs. (Farquhar et al., Nature 630:625-630, 2024)

## S-009 (general)

A supervised estimator trained on labeled data and hidden activations beats entropy baselines for detecting wrong answers in LLMs, works in black/grey/white-box settings, and better uncertainty estimation leads to better calibration performance. (Liu et al., arXiv 2404.15993; verified abstract)

## S-010 (general)

Sampling multiple reasoning paths and taking the most consistent answer (self-consistency) improves accuracy on arithmetic/commonsense benchmarks (GSM8K +17.9%). (Wang et al., ICLR 2023; verified abstract)

## S-011 (general)

Opinion (synthesis): the cross-sample agreement produced by self-consistency is what entropy/ensemble UQ methods (semantic entropy, supervised hidden-state estimators) build on — the connection is the premise of semantic entropy's method, which measures dispersion across samples.

## S-012 (general)

A statistically calibrated generative LM must hallucinate on 'arbitrary' facts appearing once in the training data, at a rate bounded below by a Good-Turing estimate, independent of architecture or data quality; mitigation requires post-training or selective behavior, not better calibration alone. (Kalai & Vempala, STOC 2024; verified abstract)

## S-013 (general)

Opinion (synthesis from the above): confidence display is insufficient on its own — abstention/verification is required; the paper states post-training mitigation is needed, the display implication is synthesis.

## S-014 (general)

LLM uncertainty-quantification methods (predictive-distribution, representation, ensemble-based) 'perform well under the restrictive assumption of no ambiguity [but] degrade to close-to-random performance on ambiguous data' — they conflate epistemic and aleatoric (ambiguous) uncertainty. (Tomov et al., arXiv 2511.04418; verified abstract)

## S-015 (general)

Opinion (derived): this ambiguity failure limits how much operators should trust displayed confidence on genuinely ambiguous inputs.

## S-016 (general)

Showing a model's confidence score alone calibrated people's trust and improved their performance in a mortgage-lending decision task; local explanations did not reliably add benefit, and improved trust calibration was not sufficient for accuracy gains — experts still had to bring knowledge complementary to the AI's errors. (Zhang, Liao, Bellamy 2020, FAT*)

## S-017 (general)

In large-scale image classification (N=1,152,000 observations), machine confidence scores and human confidence ratings each independently and significantly improved hybrid human+AI accuracy (machine confidence log-odds effect 0.257, human confidence 0.104, class error model 0.101; all p<.001); human confidence ratings were as effective as an explicit error model. (Steyvers et al., PNAS 119(11), 2022; full text verified)

## S-018 (general)

Complementarity has narrow preconditions: a hybrid human+AI pair beats both human-only and machine-only ensembles only when the two classifiers perform within a bounded accuracy band, and the key limiting factor is the latent correlation between human and machine confidence (observed ~0.4 for human+AI vs ~0.7 human-human); effective AI advice should be accurate and statistically independent of human judgment. (Steyvers et al., PNAS 2022)

## S-019 (general)

In a 199-participant study, adding explanations to AI recommendations did not reduce acceptance of wrong advice; forcing deliberative engagement ('cognitive forcing') did reduce overreliance but was the least-liked design and helped high 'Need for Cognition' users more. (Buçinca et al., CSCW 2021; verified abstract)

## S-020 (general)

Explanations only engender appropriate reliance and complementary performance insofar as they let the operator check the AI prediction's correctness; most explanations and most tasks do not enable this verification. (Fok & Weld, arXiv 2305.07722; verified abstract)

## S-021 (general)

Uncertainty-based abstention measurably improves deployed-quality outcomes: filtering out semantically uncertain questions in a clinical radiology VLM raised answer accuracy on retained questions from 51.7% to 76.3% (GPT-4o) and 54.8% to 63.8% (GPT-4.1), both p<.001. (Wienholt et al., Eur Radiol 2026, arXiv 2510.09256; verified abstract)

## S-022 (general)

An adaptive conformal semantic-entropy score with an accept/abstain decision rule carries a finite-sample, distribution-free bound on error rate among accepted answers (TriviaQA AUROC 0.88 vs 0.65 for token entropy) — conformal-guaranteed abstention is deployable. (Karimi et al., IJCAI 2026, arXiv 2605.04295; verified abstract)

## S-023 (general)

No first-party product documentation describing deployed operator-facing confidence UIs (e.g., what ChatGPT/Claude/Vertex actually render for confidence in production) was located; the production picture is reconstructed from methods papers, first-party lab research (OpenAI Lin 2022, Anthropic Kadavath 2022), and deployment-style clinical studies — an absence-of-evidence claim, not proof of non-existence (unverified).

## S-024 (general)

Established multi-source finding: showing a calibrated confidence score helps operators when the score is the operative mechanism — it improved trust calibration (Zhang 2020), machine and human confidence each significantly improved hybrid human+AI accuracy within a narrow accuracy band (Steyvers 2022), and uncertainty-based abstention improved deployed clinical accuracy ~20-25 points (Wienholt 2026).

## S-025 (general)

Established multi-source finding: explanations are a weak lever on their own — they do not reduce overreliance (Buçinca 2021) and only help when they enable verification (Fok & Weld 2024).

## S-026 (general)

Established multi-source finding: hard limits — calibrated LMs must hallucinate single-occurrence facts (Kalai & Vempala 2024) and UQ degrades under genuine ambiguity (Tomov 2025) — so confidence display must be paired with abstention/verification.

## S-027 (general)

Unverified from sources reached: how and whether specific deployed vendors (ChatGPT, Claude, Vertex) present confidence to operators in production; several key claims (F7 verbalized confidence, F8 P(True), F11 self-consistency, F12 calibrated-must-hallucinate) rest on author abstracts rather than full texts; full texts of Lee & See 2004, Dietvorst 2015, and Yáñez 2026 were not read (title/metadata level only).
