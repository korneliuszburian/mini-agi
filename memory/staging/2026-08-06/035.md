## S-000 (software-engineering)

Architecture Decision Records (ADR): a short text file recording one 'architecturally significant' decision with sections Title, Context, Decision, Status, Consequences; records numbered sequentially and monotonically, never reused; a reversed decision is kept but marked 'superseded' with a reference to its replacement; kept in version control under doc/arch/adr-NNN.md.

## S-001 (software-engineering)

ADR rationale preservation purpose: without it a new team member can only 'blindly accept' or 'blindly change' a past decision; 'the motivation behind previous decisions is visible for everyone, present and future.'

## S-002 (software-engineering)

Kubernetes Enhancement Proposals (KEP): a proposal-format process with 'a clear process with approvers and reviewers for making decisions,' giving 'a discoverable record around the decisions'; each KEP is numbered by its tracking issue and required for most non-trivial changes.

## S-003 (security)

OWASP application-log content guidance: 'The application logs must record when, where, who and what for each event,' with per-attribute fields including event type, severity, interaction identifier, user identity, action, object, result status, and reason.

## S-004 (security)

OWASP log separation: process monitoring, audit, and transaction logs/trails are usually collected for different purposes than security event logging, and this often means they should be kept separate.

## S-005 (security)

OWASP: plain logs have weak non-repudiation — 'non-repudiation is hard to achieve for logs because their trustworthiness is often just based on the logging party being audited properly while mechanisms like digital signatures are hard to utilize here.'

## S-006 (security)

OWASP log integrity at rest requires tamper detection, read-only media 'as soon as possible', restricted and monitored access; event data from other trust zones must be treated as untrusted.

## S-007 (data-provenance)

W3C PROV defines provenance as 'information about entities, activities, and people involved in producing a piece of data or thing, which can be used to form assessments about its quality, reliability or trustworthiness'; the family covers reproducibility, versioning, attribution, processing steps, and derivation; PROV-DM is the conceptual model, PROV-O its OWL2/RDF serialization.

## S-008 (supply-chain-security)

in-toto (CNCF graduated project) 'is designed to ensure the integrity of a software product from initiation to end-user installation… making it transparent to the user what steps were performed, by whom and in what order.'

## S-009 (supply-chain-security)

in-toto attestation Statement is the layer 'binding it to a particular subject and unambiguously identifying the types of the Predicate'; every subject element MUST have digest set (subjects matched purely by digest), and policies should be monotonic (ignoring an attestation/field must never turn DENY into ALLOW).

## S-010 (supply-chain-security)

SLSA provenance is 'an attestation that a particular build platform produced a set of software artifacts through execution of the buildDefinition'; the model separates buildDefinition (buildType, untrusted externalParameters, trusted internalParameters, resolvedDependencies with digests) from runDetails (builder.id, invocationId, timestamps, byproducts); 'Consumers MUST accept only specific signer-builder pairs.'

## S-011 (verifiable-credentials)

W3C VC Data Integrity: cryptographic proofs make 'documents and data tamper-evident'; Proof Sets are unordered multiple signatures, Proof Chains preserve order via previousProof (spec example: a notary counter-signing), and proofPurpose constrains what a proof may be used for.

## S-012 (regulation)

EU AI Act Art 26(2): deployers of high-risk systems 'shall assign human oversight to natural persons who have the necessary competence, training and authority'; for biometric identification, no action or decision may be taken on the basis of the identification unless separately verified and confirmed by at least two natural persons, and the separate verifications 'could be sufficient… automatically recorded in the logs generated by the system' (recital 72).

## S-013 (regulation)

EU AI Act Art 26(10): post-remote biometric identification 'shall request an authorisation, ex ante… by a judicial authority or an administrative authority whose decision is binding and subject to judicial review'; if the authorisation is rejected, use stops immediately and the linked personal data is deleted.

## S-014 (software-engineering)

The KEP process's approver/reviewer approval is itself the recorded gate (approvers and reviewers for making decisions; a discoverable record), i.e., an approval step persisted as part of the decision record.

## S-015 (verifiable-credentials)

VC Data Integrity proof sets/chains are a standards-level mechanism for multi-party signing of one document (unordered co-signatures vs. ordered counter-signatures) — the substrate approval records could be built on.

## S-016 (regulation)

EU AI Act Art 12(1): 'High-risk AI systems shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system'; logging must enable recording events relevant to identifying risk situations or substantial modification, post-market monitoring, and monitoring operation; for biometric systems minimum logged fields include the period of each use (start/end time), the reference database checked, input data that produced a match, and identification of the natural persons involved in verifying results (Art 12(2)-(3)).

## S-017 (regulation)

EU AI Act retention: providers must keep logs 'for a period appropriate to the intended purpose… of at least six months' (Art 19(1)); deployers have the same six-month minimum (Art 26(6)); financial institutions keep them as part of internal-governance documentation.

## S-018 (regulation)

EU AI Act access: upon a reasoned request, providers must give competent authorities access to the automatically generated logs (Art 21(2)).

## S-019 (regulation)

EU AI Act transparency: high-risk systems must be 'sufficiently transparent to enable deployers to interpret a system's output and use it appropriately,' and instructions for use must state capabilities and limitations, known risks, and human-oversight measures (Art 13).

## S-020 (regulation)

NIST AI RMF 1.0 (released 26 Jan 2023, voluntary) organizes risk management into four functions — Govern, Map, Measure, Manage — with a companion Playbook of suggested actions per sub-category.

## S-021 (regulation)

AI RMF Playbook Govern: GOVERN 1.1 'Legal and regulatory requirements involving AI are understood, managed, and documented'; GOVERN 1.4 urges standardized documentation (AI-actor contact info, business justification, scope and usages, expected risks, assumptions and limitations, training-data characterization, algorithmic methodology, testing and validation results, dependencies, deployment/monitoring/change-management plans) plus a 'model documentation inventory'; GOVERN 1.5 covers ongoing monitoring, incident response, and 'appeal and override' (human adjudication of system outcomes); GOVERN 2.1 requires roles, responsibilities, and lines of communication to be 'documented and clear.'

## S-025 (ai-auditability)

Opinion: the three mechanisms answer different audit questions — decision logs answer what was decided and why (rationale + when/who/what fields); evidence chains answer what produced this result and can it be re-verified (attribution + derivation + digests, tamper-evident); approval records answer who authorized it (a human gate persisted as a record).

## S-026 (ai-auditability)

Opinion: in the EU AI Act these layers are combined by law, not alternatives — Art 12 mandates an automatic decision log; Art 14/26 require that human-oversight and biometric authorisation events be captured (as approvals inside the logs, recital 72); Art 19/26(6) set retention; Art 21 gives authorities log access.

## S-027 (ai-auditability)

Opinion: the weakness of a bare decision log is integrity and non-repudiation — OWASP states plain logs cannot reliably provide it without signatures; the evidence-chain standards supply exactly the missing machinery (signed Statements bound to subjects by digest, provenance predicates, proof chains). The suggested shape is a decision log whose entries embed digest references bound by an attestation/evidence chain, with approval records as human-signed gates on top — not one mechanism chosen over the others.

## S-028 (ai-auditability)

Established: each mechanism is a real, standards-/law-backed practice with distinct content — decision logs (ADR/KEP rationale + versioning, OWASP when/who/what fields and separate audit trails), evidence chains (PROV attribution/derivation; in-toto/SLSA digest-bound signed attestations; VC Data Integrity proof sets/chains and proof purposes), approval records (EU AI Act mandatory human-oversight and biometric-authorisation approvals recorded in system logs with six-month retention; NIST AI RMF Playbook documented roles, standardized model documentation, and inventory).

## S-029 (ai-auditability)

Uncertain: no single standard prescribes how to combine all three mechanisms for AI decision auditability — no primary source reached does; it is legal text plus practice guides, not one integrated spec.
