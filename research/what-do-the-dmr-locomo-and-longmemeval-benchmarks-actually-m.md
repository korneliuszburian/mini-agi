## Findings

### What each benchmark measures

**DMR — Deep Memory Retrieval (from MemGPT)**

- **Origin and definition (fact):** DMR is a task introduced in the MemGPT paper, built on the Multi-Session Chat (MSC) dataset. "We introduce a new 'deep memory retrieval' (DMR) task based on the MSC dataset designed to test the consistency of a conversational agent. In DMR, the conversational agent is asked a question by the user that explicitly refers back to a prior conversation and has a very narrow expected answer range." QA pairs are generated by a separate LLM instructed to write a question "that can only be answered by looking at the old chat log (and is not contained in the persona information)." — *MemGPT: Towards LLMs as Operating Systems*, Packer et al., arXiv:2310.08560 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560).
- **Scale/setup (fact):** MSC conversations have five sessions of ~12 messages; a new session 6 containing one QA pair was added. The Zep paper reports DMR as "a 500-conversation subset of the Multi-Session Chat dataset." Metrics are ROUGE-L plus an LLM judge scoring CORRECT/WRONG. Reported accuracies: GPT-3.5 Turbo 38.7% → MemGPT 66.9%; GPT-4 32.1% → 92.5%; GPT-4 Turbo 35.3% → 93.4%. — *MemGPT* arXiv:2310.08560; *Zep*, Rasmussen et al., arXiv:2501.13956 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13956).
- **What it actually measures (opinion, grounded in the above):** single-session-hop fact recall — whether an agent can pull one specific fact out of a prior session. It tests retrieval/consistency, not updating, temporal reasoning, or abstention. The Zep paper makes the same critique in primary text: "The evaluation relies exclusively on single-turn, fact-retrieval questions... Each conversation contains only 60 messages, easily fitting within current LLM context windows." — *Zep* arXiv:2501.13956.

**LoCoMo**

- **Definition (fact):** "we collect LoCoMo, a dataset of very long-term conversations, each encompassing 300 turns and 9K tokens on avg., over up to 35 sessions," 50 dialogues, generated via a machine-human pipeline grounded in personas and temporal event graphs, including multimodal (image-sharing) turns. — *Evaluating Very Long-Term Conversational Memory of LLM Agents*, Maharana et al., arXiv:2402.17753 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17753).
- **Task suite (fact):** three tasks: (1) QA in five categories — single-hop, multi-hop, temporal reasoning, open-domain (commonsense/world) knowledge, and adversarial ("designed to trick the agent into providing wrong answers, with the expectation that the agent will correctly identify them as unanswerable"); (2) event-graph summarization scored with FactScore against the ground-truth temporal event graph; (3) multi-modal dialogue generation scored with MMRelevance. — *LoCoMo* arXiv:2402.17753.
- **Measured behaviors (fact):** temporal and causal comprehension over long horizons; hallucination/abstention resistance (adversarial). Findings: long-context LLMs/RAG improve QA 22–66% but "significantly lag behind human levels (by 56%), especially in temporal reasoning (by 73%)"; long-context models are "especially prone to misassigning dialogs or events to the wrong speaker"; gpt-3.5-turbo-16k scored 2.1% on adversarial questions. — *LoCoMo* arXiv:2402.17753. RoMem later classifies LoCoMo as the "hybrid" benchmark: "a mixture of dynamic temporal updates and general knowledge queries." — *Time is Not a Label* (RoMem), arXiv:2604.11544 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11544).

**LongMemEval**

- **Definition (fact):** 500 manually curated questions over task-oriented user-assistant histories, evaluating five abilities: information extraction (IE), multi-session reasoning (MR), temporal reasoning (TR), knowledge updates (KU), and abstention (ABS). Seven question types; history length freely scalable, with two standard settings — LongMemEval_S (~115k tokens/question) and LongMemEval_M (500 sessions, ~1.5M tokens). — *LongMemEval*, Wu et al., arXiv:2410.10813, ICLR 2025 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813).
- **KU definition (fact):** "Ability to recognize the changes in the user's personal information and update the knowledge of the user dynamically over time." KU error analysis states the retriever "was only able to identify the updated knowledge but failed to retrieve the previous information before update," and that this is counted as retrieval failure. — *LongMemEval* arXiv:2410.10813 (Appendix E.5).
- **Measured behaviors (fact):** the benchmark is hard: long-context LLMs drop 30–60% vs. oracle retrieval on LongMemEval_S; commercial ChatGPT and Coze dropped 37% and 64% vs. offline reading. "ChatGPT tended to overwrite crucial information as the chat continues, while Coze often failed to record indirectly provided user information." — *LongMemEval* arXiv:2410.10813 (§3.4, Appendix B).

### Where append-only fact stores typically fail

- **On knowledge-update and temporal reasoning (LongMemEval):** the LongMemEval authors report "Naive time-agnostic memory designs perform poorly on temporal reasoning questions" — time-aware indexing/query expansion improves temporal-reasoning recall by 6.8–11.3% — and that KU questions require retrieving *both* the pre-update and post-update evidence, with a store that drops the older fact counted as failed retrieval. — *LongMemEval* arXiv:2410.10813 (§5.4, Appendix E.5).
- **Static-dynamic dilemma (the canonical failure):** RoMem's primary text formalizes it: "most existing approaches model time as discrete metadata, either sorting by recency (burying old-yet-permanent knowledge), simply overwriting outdated facts, or requiring an expensive LLM call at every ingestion step, leaving them unable to distinguish persistent facts from evolving ones." Two failure directions: (a) recency sorting "silently buries static knowledge: a recency-based system ranks the decades-old fact (Obama, born_in, Hawaii) below fresher but irrelevant entries"; (b) "Disabling recency bias leaves temporal conflicts unresolved, confusing the downstream LLM." RoMem asserts its append-only store only works because contradictions are resolved geometrically at query time ("The memory remains strictly append-only, yet the LLM receives a clean, unambiguous context window"), and claims "All existing baselines including Mem0, Zep, LicoMemory, and HippoRAG, treat memory as a static snapshot, causing contradictory facts to cluster together in the retrieval space and confuse the LLM." — *RoMem* arXiv:2604.11544 (§1, §3, §4.3).
- **On LoCoMo temporal-reasoning (author-reported numbers, single source):** RoMem reports baselines Mem0 temporal 0.690, HippoRAG 0.645, Zep 0.553, A-Mem 0.691 on LoCoMo Recall@10 (GPT-5-mini impl.), vs. RoMem 0.726; and on DMR-MSC, naive-receny handling is claimed to be "precisely the behaviour that naive recency-based approaches would destroy" for static fact retrieval (Mem0 MRR 0.847, HippoRAG 0.848, RoMem 0.856). — *RoMem* arXiv:2604.11544 (Table 2).
- **On detail-level information extraction:** fact-extraction compression trades recall of detail. "While further compression into individual user facts harms overall performance due to information loss, it improves the multi-session reasoning accuracy." — *LongMemEval* arXiv:2410.10813 (Abstract; §5.2). LoCoMo likewise: session-summary RAG does not improve QA "likely due to loss of information during the conversion of dialogs to summaries." — *LoCoMo* arXiv:2402.17753.
- **On adversarial/abstention:** facts never stored can't be abstained on — Coze "often failed to record indirectly provided user information" (implicitly-stated facts never enter the store), and compressed/overwriting stores corrupt prior facts over time (ChatGPT). — *LongMemEval* arXiv:2410.10813 (Appendix B). LoCoMo's adversarial category (2.1% for a long-context model) is the same failure surface. — *LoCoMo* arXiv:2402.17753.
- **What append-only stores do NOT fail on — DMR:** DMR is precisely the task where a static fact store succeeds; it has no update, temporal, or abstention categories, and the Zep paper shows a plain full-conversation baseline already matches MemGPT (94.4% vs 93.4%, both gpt-4-turbo; Zep 94.8%). Hence high DMR scores co-exist with failures on LongMemEval/LoCoMo. — *Zep* arXiv:2501.13956 (§4.2).

## Verdict

**Established:** DMR (MemGPT, 2023) measures single-session-hop fact recall on short 5-session MSC chats and is near-saturated — a full-context baseline already matches the memory system (Zep: 93.4–94.8%). LoCoMo (2024) measures long-horizon (up to 35 sessions, ~9K tokens, multimodal) QA across single/multi-hop, temporal, open-domain, and adversarial questions plus event-summarization, and its own results show temporal reasoning and abstention are the hardest axes (human 87.9 F1 vs. best model ~37.8; long-context adversarial 2.1%). LongMemEval (ICLR 2025) explicitly measures IE, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates, and abstention at 115k–1.5M-token scales, and documents 30–60% drops for long-context LLMs and 37–64% drops for commercial memory assistants.

**On append-only fact stores:** the primary sources converge on where they fail: (1) knowledge-update — LongMemEval's KU category requires the pre-update evidence to *also* be retrieved; a store that drops or fails to surface the old fact fails (its error analysis counts exactly this as a failure); (2) temporal reasoning — "naive time-agnostic memory designs perform poorly" and time-aware indexing yields +6.8–11.3% recall (LongMemEval §5.4); (3) the static-dynamic dilemma — RoMem's primary text documents that recency-sorted append-only stores bury static facts, while unresolvable contradictions "cluster together in the retrieval space and confuse the LLM" (§1, §4.3); (4) abstention/adversarial — never-stored or overwritten facts can't be abstained on (LongMemEval Appendix B; LoCoMo adversarial results). DMR is the one benchmark where append-only fact stores are at home — which is why a system can score well on DMR while failing the other two.

**Uncertain/estimatED:** RoMem's cross-benchmark numbers (LoCoMo Recall@10 per-category; DMR-MSC MRR for Mem0/HippoRAG/Zep) are single-paper, author-reported, unpublished elsewhere, and use a different metric than the original LoCoMo paper (Recall@10 over ~1,986 QA pairs vs. the original paper's F1 answer prediction over 50 dialogues); the LoCoMo question count differs across sources (1,813 in one 2026 paper vs. 1,986 in RoMem). Zep's LongMemEval improvements (up to +18.5% accuracy, −90% latency) are vendor self-reported. No primary source I reached gives a controlled head-to-head of a "pure" append-only store vs. an updating store on all three benchmarks with an identical LLM judge.

**Evidence that would settle it:** an independent evaluation running the same underlying model against (a) a plain append-only fact/vector store, (b) an updating/invalidating store (e.g., temporal KG), and (c) full-context reading, on LongMemEval (per question-type: KU, TR, ABS) and LoCoMo (temporal, adversarial) with a fixed judge. The LongMemEval repo (github.com/xiaowu0162/LongMemEval) and RoMem's reported protocol are the two ready-made harnesses for that test.
