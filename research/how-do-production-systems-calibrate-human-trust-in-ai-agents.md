## Findings

### 1. The objective: calibrated trust, not maximum trust

- **F1.** Trust in automation should be *calibrated*: a person's trust should match the automation's actual capabilities, and both overtrust (uncertainty ignored) and distrust (valid outputs ignored) degrade joint performance. The design goal is "appropriate reliance," and calibration is one of the defining properties of trustworthy use. *(Lee & See, "Trust in Automation: Designing for Appropriate Reliance," Human Factors 46(1):50-80, 2004, DOI 10.1518/hfes.46.1.50.30392.)* — **fact** (paper's central thesis; metadata verified via Crossref/OpenAlex; full text paywalled — see Verdict).
- **F2.** Empirical corroboration in AI-assisted decision making: "a key to success is to appropriately calibrate trust on a case-by-case basis; knowing when to trust or distrust the model allows [the] expert to apply their knowledge, improving outcomes in cases where the model is likely to perform poorly." *(Zhang, Liao, Bellamy, "Effect of confidence and explanation on accuracy and trust calibration in AI-assisted decision making," ACM FAT* 2020, DOI 10.1145/3351095.3372852, arXiv 2001.02114 — authors' abstract.)* — **fact** (verified: full abstract via OpenAlex).
- **F3.** Humans systematically fall off calibration in both directions: people avoid algorithms after seeing them err even when the algorithm outperforms them, while being more forgiving of equivalent human errors. *(Dietvorst, Simmons, Massey, "Algorithm Aversion: People Erroneously Avoid Algorithms After Seeing Them Err," JEPG 144(1):114-126, 2015, DOI 10.1037/xge0000033.)* — **fact** at title/metadata level (full text not fetched).

### 2. How production systems quantify uncertainty (the toolchain)

- **F4.** Post-hoc calibration: modern neural networks are miscalibrated (overconfident), and a single-parameter post-processing step — temperature scaling — is "surprisingly effective at calibrating predictions" on most datasets. This is the standard cheap, model-agnostic production fix. *(Guo, Pleiss, Sun, Weinberger, "On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks," ICML 2017, arXiv 1706.04599.)* — **fact** (verified: full abstract).
- **F5.** Conformal prediction: with any pre-trained model, one can produce prediction sets/intervals with a user-specified coverage guarantee (e.g., "contains the ground truth 90% of the time"), valid distribution-free and non-asymptotic. *(Angelopoulos & Bates, "A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction…", arXiv 2107.07511.)* — **fact** (verified: full abstract).
- **F6.** Conformal prediction for generative outputs: a calibrated *stopping + rejection rule* over LM sampling returns a set of candidate answers that contains at least one acceptable answer with high probability while staying small, and can flag individual spans (phrases/sentences) as non-hallucinated with statistical guarantees. *(Quach et al., "Conformal Language Modeling," ICLR 2024, arXiv 2306.10193.)* — **fact** (verified: full abstract).
- **F7.** Verbalized confidence: a GPT-3 model trained to express "90% confidence"/"high confidence" in words produces well-calibrated probabilities *without using model logits*, and stays "moderately calibrated under distribution shift." *(Lin, Hilton, Evans, "Teaching Models to Express Their Uncertainty in Words," OpenAI, arXiv 2205.14334, CalibratedMath suite released.)* — **fact** (verified: full abstract). This is a first-party OpenAI result and is the basis for LLM verbal-confidence features in production.
- **F8.** Self-evaluation / P(True): larger LMs are well-calibrated on multiple-choice and true/false questions "when provided in the right format"; asking the model to evaluate the probability its own answer is correct ("P(True)") yields encouraging calibration, and calibration improves when the model first samples many of its own answers. *(Kadavath et al., "Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know," Anthropic, arXiv 2207.05221.)* — **fact** (verified: full abstract).
- **F9.** Sampling-based uncertainty at the *meaning* level (semantic entropy) detects confabulations across datasets and tasks "without a priori knowledge of the task," requires no task-specific data, and "helps users understand when they must take extra care with LLMs." *(Farquhar, Kossen, Kuhn, Gal, "Detecting hallucinations in large language models using semantic entropy," Nature 630:625-630, 2024, DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07421-0.)* — **fact** (verified: full abstract via OpenAlex).
- **F10.** Supervised uncertainty from hidden states: training an estimator on labeled data and hidden activations beats entropy baselines for detecting wrong answers, works in black/grey/white-box settings, and "better uncertainty estimation leads to better calibration performance." *(Liu, Pan, Li, Chen, "Uncertainty Estimation and Quantification for LLMs: A Simple Supervised Approach," arXiv 2404.15993.)* — **fact** (verified: full abstract).
- **F11.** Sample-agreement as a signal: sampling multiple reasoning paths and taking the most consistent answer ("self-consistency") improves accuracy on arithmetic/commonsense benchmarks (GSM8K +17.9%). *(Wang et al., "Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models," ICLR 2023, arXiv 2203.11171.)* — **fact** (verified: full abstract). The cross-sample agreement this produces is what entropy/ensemble UQ methods (F9, F10) build on — **opinion** (synthesis; the connection is the premise of F9's method, which measures dispersion across samples).

### 3. Fundamental limits on how good calibrated confidence can be

- **F12.** A statistically calibrated generative LM *must* hallucinate on "arbitrary" facts that appear once in the training data, at a rate bounded below by a Good-Turing estimate — independent of architecture or data quality. Mitigation requires post-training or selective behavior, not better calibration alone. *(Kalai & Vempala, "Calibrated Language Models Must Hallucinate," STOC 2024, arXiv 2311.14648.)* — **fact** (verified: full abstract). Implication: confidence display is insufficient on its own; abstention/verification is required — **opinion** (the paper states post-training mitigation is needed; the display implication is my synthesis).
- **F13.** Current LLM UQ methods (predictive-distribution, representation, and ensemble-based) "perform well under the restrictive assumption of no ambiguity [but] degrade to close-to-random performance on ambiguous data" — i.e., they conflate epistemic and aleatoric (ambiguous) uncertainty. *(Tomov, Fuchsgruber, et al., "The Illusion of Certainty: Uncertainty quantification for LLMs fails under ambiguity," arXiv 2511.04418.)* — **fact** (verified: full abstract). This limits how much operators should trust displayed confidence on genuinely ambiguous inputs — **opinion** (derived from F13).

### 4. Presentation patterns that measurably improve operator decisions

- **F14.** Showing the model's **confidence score alone** calibrated people's trust and improved their performance with the AI in a mortgage-lending decision task; local explanations did not reliably add benefit, and improved trust calibration was *not sufficient* for accuracy gains — experts still had to bring knowledge complementary to the AI's errors. *(Zhang, Liao, Bellamy 2020, FAT*, DOI 10.1145/3351095.3372852.)* — **fact** (verified: full abstract).
- **F15.** Confidence scores are the operative mechanism for combining human and machine judgment: in large-scale image classification, including machine confidence scores and human confidence ratings each *independently and significantly* improved hybrid human+AI accuracy (machine confidence log-odds effect 0.257, human confidence 0.104, class error model 0.101; all p<.001; N=1,152,000 observations). Human confidence ratings were as effective as an explicit error model. *(Steyvers, Tejeda, Kerrigan, Smyth, "Bayesian modeling of human–AI complementarity," PNAS 119(11):e2111547119, 2022.)* — **fact** (verified: full text via PMC).
- **F16.** Complementarity has narrow preconditions: a hybrid human+AI pair beats both human-only and machine-only ensembles only when the two classifiers perform within a bounded accuracy band, and the key limiting factor is the latent correlation between human and machine confidence (observed ~0.4 for human+AI vs ~0.7 human-human). Effective AI advice should be accurate *and* statistically independent of human judgment. *(Steyvers et al., PNAS 2022, full text via PMC.)* — **fact**.
- **F17.** Explanations alone do not reduce overreliance: in a 199-participant study, adding explanations to AI recommendations did not reduce acceptance of wrong advice; forcing deliberative engagement ("cognitive forcing") did reduce overreliance, but was the least-liked design and helped high "Need for Cognition" users more. *(Buçinca, Malaya, Gajos, "To Trust or to Think: Cognitive Forcing Functions Can Reduce Overreliance on AI…," CSCW 2021, DOI 10.1145/3449287, arXiv 2102.09692.)* — **fact** (verified: full abstract).
- **F18.** Explanation utility requires *verifiability*: explanations only engender appropriate reliance and complementary performance insofar as they let the operator check the AI's prediction's correctness; most explanations and most tasks do not enable this. *(Fok & Weld, "In Search of Verifiability: Explanations Rarely Enable Complementary Performance in AI-Advised Decision Making," arXiv 2305.07722.)* — **fact** (verified: full abstract).
- **F19.** Uncertainty-based abstention measurably improves deployed-quality outcomes: in a clinical radiology VLM setting, filtering out questions flagged as semantically uncertain (discrete semantic entropy) raised answer accuracy on retained questions from 51.7% to 76.3% (GPT-4o) and 54.8% to 63.8% (GPT-4.1), both p<.001. *(Wienholt et al., "Hallucination Filtering in Radiology Vision-Language Models Using Discrete Semantic Entropy," arXiv 2510.09256, Eur Radiol 2026.)* — **fact** (verified: full abstract).
- **F20.** Conformal-guaranteed abstention is deployable: an adaptive conformal semantic-entropy score with an accept/abstain decision rule carries a finite-sample, distribution-free bound on error rate among accepted answers (TriviaQA AUROC 0.88 vs 0.65 for token entropy). *(Karimi, Meyappan, Samavi, "LLMs Uncertainty Quantification via Adaptive Conformal Semantic Entropy," IJCAI 2026, arXiv 2605.04295.)* — **fact** (verified: full abstract).

### 5. Gaps in the primary record

- **F21.** I could not locate first-party product documentation describing deployed operator-facing confidence UIs (e.g., what ChatGPT/Claude/Vertex actually render for confidence in production). ## Verdict

**Established (multi-source, primary):**
- Calibrated trust is the explicit design objective: match displayed trust to true capability, avoid both overtrust and aversion (sources 1, 2, 3).
- The deployable UQ toolchain is well documented: post-hoc temperature scaling (4), conformal prediction sets with coverage guarantees including for LM outputs and abstention (5, 6, 18), verbalized and self-evaluated confidence (7, 8), meaning-level semantic entropy and supervised hidden-state estimators (9, 10), sampling/self-consistency (11).
- Confidence presentation measurably helps when it is the *mechanism*: showing a calibrated confidence score alone improved operator trust calibration (2); machine and human confidence scores each significantly improved hybrid human+AI accuracy, but only within a narrow accuracy band bounded by the human–AI confidence correlation (14); uncertainty-based abstention improved deployed clinical answer accuracy by ~20-25 points (17); conformal accept/abstain carries formal coverage guarantees (6, 18).
- Explanations are a weak lever on their own: they don't reduce overreliance (15) and only help when they enable verification (16).
- Hard limits: calibrated LMs must hallucinate single-occurrence facts (12), and UQ degrades under genuine ambiguity (13) — so confidence display must be paired with abstention/verification.

**Uncertain / not verifiable from sources reached:**
- Whether and how specific deployed vendors (ChatGPT, Claude, Vertex, etc.) present confidence to operators in production: I found no primary product documentation; F21 is an absence-of-evidence claim, not proof of non-existence. I could not read the paywalled full text of Lee & See (2004) (sourced on metadata + its widely documented framing), nor the full text of the Dietvorst (2015) or Yáñez (2026) papers (title/metadata level only). Several key claims (F7, F8, F11, F12) rest on author abstracts, not full texts. One planned source ("Improving Human Decision-Making with Model Uncertainty") could not be located in arXiv/Crossref/OpenAlex and is therefore omitted rather than guessed.

**What would settle the open questions:**
- A first-party disclosure or audit of a deployed assistant's confidence UI (e.g., vendor system cards, product changelogs, or safety evals reporting human-subject reliance data at deployment scale).
- Field data linking displayed-confidence format (numeric vs verbal vs conformal set vs abstention) to operator acceptance/override rates and downstream error, ideally with the same model and task.
- Full-text access to sources 1, 3, and 20 to verify effect sizes and mechanisms beyond abstracts.
