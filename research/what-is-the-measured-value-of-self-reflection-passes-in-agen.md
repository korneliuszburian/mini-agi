## Findings

### 1. Reflexion: the measured value of a verbal self-reflection loop

- **Fact** — Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2023) reports pass@1 on HumanEval of **91%** vs. **80%** for the then-SOTA GPT-4 baseline, an **+11pp** gain from the trial–error–reflection loop. Source: Reflexion abstract, arXiv:2303.11366v4 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366).
- **Fact** — On ALFWorld (134 text-world tasks), ReAct+Reflexion completed **130/134** tasks vs. ReAct-only which stalled; the paper reports an **absolute +22%** improvement over strong baselines within 12 iterative trials. Source: Reflexion §4.1 (https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11366v4).
- **Fact** — On HotpotQA, Reflexion improved over baselines by **+20%**; with ground-truth context (CoT-GT), Reflexion lifted accuracy by **14%** without access to ground-truth answers. Source: Reflexion §4.2 (https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11366v4).
- **Fact** — The paper's own ablation isolates the reflection step. On the 50 hardest HumanEval Rust problems (GPT-4 base): base **0.60**; self-reflection *without* test execution **0.52**; test execution *without* the natural-language reflection **0.60**; full Reflexion (tests + reflection) **0.68**. So the reflection pass added +8pp *only when paired with an external evaluator*, and the "reflect but no tests" variant performed worse than the base model. Source: Reflexion §4.3, Table 3.
- **Fact** — On HotpotQA, an episodic-memory-only ablation (recent trajectory, no reflection) gained less than the full reflection step, which added an **8% absolute** boost — i.e., the verbal reflection step, not just feeding back the failed trace, carried the gain. Source: Reflexion §4.2 Analysis.
- **Fact** — A documented case where the reflection loop *hurt*: on MBPP (Python), Reflexion scored **77.1** vs. the GPT-4 baseline's **80.1** pass@1. The paper attributes this to flaky self-generated unit tests: the false-positive rate of the internal test suite was **16.3%** on MBPP vs. **1.4%** on HumanEval, so the agent prematurely accepted wrong submissions. Source: Reflexion §4.3, Tables 1–2.

### 2. Reflection/verification designs that measurably improve reliability

- **Fact** — ReAct (Yao et al., 2023), which interleaves sparse "thought" traces with environment actions, outperformed imitation and RL baselines by **+34%** (ALFWorld) and **+10%** (WebShop) absolute success rate; on WebShop, ReAct's 40.0% SR beat Act (30.1%), IL (29.1%) and IL+RL (28.7%). Grounding reasoning in a Wikipedia API cut hallucination: among failed HotpotQA trajectories, hallucination was the cause in 56% of CoT cases vs. 0% for ReAct. Source: ReAct, arXiv:2210.03629 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629; full text §3.3 Table 2, §4).
- **Fact** — ReAct's own ablation against "Inner-Monologue"-style prompts (ReAct-IM, which replays dense *external* feedback with no internal goal reasoning) shows internal sparse reasoning beats pure external-feedback replay: **71% vs. 53%** overall ALFWorld success. This is direct evidence about *which* reflection content adds value (goal-level re-planning) vs. which does not (regurgitating observations). Source: ReAct §4 Results, https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.03629.
- **Fact** — CRITIC (Gou et al., 2023) makes the model validate its own output *against external tools* (search engine, code interpreter) and revise; the authors report it "consistently enhances the performance of LLMs" across free-form QA, mathematical program synthesis, and toxicity reduction, and conclude external feedback is "crucially important" for self-improvement. **Caveat: I could only verify the abstract** (no HTML rendering available; ar5iv conversion failed) — the per-benchmark numbers were not verified by me. Source: CRITIC abstract, arXiv:2305.11738 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11738), ICLR 2024.
- **Fact** — Tyen et al. (2024, ACL Findings) decompose self-correction: LLMs *cannot reliably find* their own reasoning errors (even in objective, unambiguous cases), but *can correct* them when given the error location; feeding ground-truth mistake-location boosts performance across 5 reasoning tasks. A small classifier trained on out-of-domain data beat prompting a large model at mistake-finding. Implication: the bottleneck is **error detection**, and external/structured detection signals are what make correction reliable. Source: arXiv:2311.08516 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08516).
- **Fact** — Training reflection into the weights (rather than prompting it) shows large gains. SPOC (2025): a single-pass interleaved solution+verification model trained by SFT + online RL improved Llama-3.1-8B/70B by **+8.8/+11.6pp** on MATH500, **+10.0/+20.0pp** on AMC23, **+3.3/+6.7pp** on AIME24. S2R (2025): teaching self-verification + self-correction via SFT and outcome/process RL lifted Qwen2.5-Math-7B from **51.0% → 81.6%** with only 3.1k seed samples. Sources: SPOC, arXiv:2506.06923 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06923); S2R, arXiv:2502.12853 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12853). Both are arXiv preprints.
- **Fact** — Retroformer (Yao et al., 2024) argues Reflexion-style verbal feedback "does not reason and plan in ways compatible with gradient-based learning"; its learned retrospective model tuned by policy gradient improves agents over time and outperforms baselines that do not use environment rewards — i.e., the *optimization of the reflection* itself is the value-adding successor step. Source: arXiv:2308.02151 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02151).

### 3. Reflection designs that add no value or degrade reliability

- **Fact** — Huang et al. (2024, ICLR) tested *intrinsic* self-correction (no external feedback) on reasoning: models "struggle to self-correct their responses without external feedback, and at times, their performance even degrades after self-correction." Source: arXiv:2310.01798 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798).
- **Fact** — Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023) reports ~20% absolute average gain across 7 tasks, **but on math reasoning (GSM8K) the self-feedback loop gained ≈0pp**: GPT-3.5 64.1→64.1, ChatGPT 74.8→75.0, GPT-4 92.9→93.1. The paper's explanation: the model cannot detect its own math errors (ChatGPT's feedback was "everything looks good" in **94%** of instances); with **oracle** (external) error feedback, the gain became **5%+**. So a reflection pass whose critic cannot detect the error adds nothing. Source: Self-Refine §3.2 Table 1, §3.3, §H.1, arXiv:2303.17651v2 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651).
- **Fact** — Self-Refine's feedback-quality ablations show actionable feedback >> generic feedback >> none (Code Optimization 27.5 vs. 26.0 vs. 24.8; Sentiment Reversal 43.2 vs. 31.2 vs. 0). And on a weak model (Vicuna-13B) the whole refinement step fails — the model cannot even emit feedback in the required format. Sources: Self-Refine §4, Table 2, and §4 "Does Self-Refine work with weaker models?".
- **Fact** — Multi-agent-debate work (Liang et al., EMNLP 2024) documents "Degeneration-of-Thought": once an LLM has established confidence, iterative self-reflection "is unable to generate novel thoughts," so reflection alone stagnates; their multi-agent debate outperformed it on commonsense translation and counter-intuitive arithmetic. They also find an LLM is "not a fair judge" across different models. Source: arXiv:2305.19118 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19118).
- **Fact** — A 2026 arXiv preprint recasts iterative self-correction as feedback control with a stability threshold (correct when error-correction-rate/error-introduction-rate exceeds a bound). Across 7 models × {GSM8K, MATH, StrategyQA}, only **o3-mini (+3.4pp), Claude Opus 4.6 (+0.6pp), o4-mini (≈0)** were non-degrading; **GPT-5 and four others lost accuracy** under repeated self-correction. A verify-first prompt intervention turned GPT-4o-mini's **−6.2pp** degradation into **+0.2pp**. This is an arXiv preprint, not yet peer-reviewed. Source: arXiv:2604.22273 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22273).
- **Fact** — Related preprint analyses reach the same conclusion from different angles: a "detection-without-correction" decomposition finds the *conditional miscorrection rate* dominant (53–94%) across model families and benchmarks (arXiv:2605.27559, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27559); and a debate/self-correction scaling study finds a noise placebo matches self-correction on free-form math at 4× scale — i.e., much of the gain attributed to "debate/reflection" may be plain re-evaluation, and 30 dense debating agents produce no more answer diversity than one (arXiv:2606.02646, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02646). Both are arXiv preprints.
- **Fact** — One preprint (2026) shows the *format/role* of the reflection matters to the point that the "models can't self-correct" result may be partly an artifact: keeping the erroneous claim byte-identical but relabeling it from the model's own `<thought>` to an external role (user message, tool response, `<memory>`) raised explicit-correction rates by **23–93 percentage points** (significant in 10/12 settings). arXiv preprint. Source: arXiv:2606.05976 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05976).

### 4. A counterpoint (context-dependence)

- **Fact** — Renze & Guven (FLLM 2024) tested 9 LLMs × 8 self-reflection agent designs on a 1,000-question multi-domain MCQA exam (ARC, AGIEval, HellaSwag, MedMCQA, …) and found *all* reflection types significantly improved re-answer accuracy (p<0.001) across all models, with information-rich reflections (Instructions, Explanation, Solution) beating minimal ones (Retry, Keywords, Advice), and even the plain "Retry" agent improving. **Important protocol caveats (from the paper itself):** reflections were generated *with the correct answer given as an external feedback signal* (answer labels redacted afterwards), the task is single-step re-answering rather than an agent loop, the LSAT-AR exam dominated the gains, and strong models near the 100% ceiling compress the effect. Source: arXiv:2405.06682 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06682). This does **not** contradict the "intrinsic self-correction fails" results — it used an external correctness signal, which the Huang et al. and Reflexion-ablations findings say is precisely what makes reflection work.

## Verdict

**Established (high confidence):**
- Reflection loops produce large, replicated gains **only when a reliable external evaluator grounds the reflection** (unit tests / environment / exact-match / tools): Reflexion +11pp HumanEval, 130/134 ALFWorld, +20% HotpotQA; ReAct +34pp/+10pp over IL baselines; CRITIC consistent gains. The Reflexion ablations pin this down directly (+8pp with tests, −8pp without).
- Pure **intrinsic** self-correction (no external feedback) on reasoning adds ≈0 or negative value: Huang et al., Self-Refine's GSM8K ≈0pp, Reflexion's test-omission ablation (0.52 < 0.60 base), and the 2026 stability-threshold preprint (only 3 of 7 tested frontier models non-degrading).
- The failure point is **error detection**, not correction: Tyen et al. (location signals restore correction), Self-Refine's "94% everything looks good," and the detection/miscorrection-decomposition preprint all converge here. Consequently, designs that externalize the critic (verifier model, tools, classifiers, verify-first prompting, or RL-trained self-verification) are the ones that improve reliability; designs that ask the generator to judge its own ungrounded reasoning add little or hurt.

**Uncertain / contested:**
- Whether "self-reflection" per se (vs. plain retry / re-sampling) contributes beyond the verification gate. Renze & Guven found even "Retry" helped; a 2026 preprint finds a noise placebo matches self-correction on math; another finds the "can't self-correct" result is partly a chat-template role-labeling artifact. These are not yet reconciled.
- Renze & Guven's positive MCQA result vs. Huang et al.'s negative reasoning result are not comparable protocols (correct-answer-conditioned reflection + re-answer vs. intrinsic correction). Neither protocol reflects a true long-horizon agent loop.

**Evidence that would settle the open points:** controlled ablations on a common agent suite varying (a) evaluator signal source (intrinsic / tool / verifier / oracle), (b) reflection content (replan vs. critique vs. retry), (c) pass count with matched compute (self-consistency / k-samples as the ceiling), and (d) role-labeling of the reflected text, reported per benchmark.

**Coverage caveats:** CRITIC was verified from its abstract only (no accessible full text); the 2025–2026 papers are arXiv preprints, not peer-reviewed. I did not verify any claim I could not read in a primary source.
